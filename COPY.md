## Copy principles

1. **Begin with the person.** Name the attempt to be understood before describing the machinery.
   Empathy and accessibility guide the writing. Consider what the reader knows, needs, and could misunderstand.
   Name ADHD and dyslexia explicitly. Respect stated preferences, attention, and working memory needs.
   Clarity, accuracy, and brevity serve that reader. Keep the context they need to understand.
   Storytelling and style help communicate meaning; keep them when they support understanding and connection.
   In the README, state what the project does, then explain the reader's problem. Keep the opening brief.
2. **Let the horror carry the loss.** The writing can become swollen with filler or lose its voice.
   Keep those images tied to a concrete reading problem. Do not add decorative horror to every paragraph.
3. **Give warmth an action.** Empathy means explaining a term or respecting the reader's attention.
   Reject vague claims that the product creates connection or makes an agent care.
4. **Keep the grotesque outside the procedure.** Public introductions can be unsettling.
   Commands, errors, and reference material use established terms and predictable steps.
   Skill names may carry the part-human pair. Each Skill description stays plain, so agents choose the right one.
5. **Keep the author's experience true.** Use supplied stories or clearly framed reader situations.
   Never invent disappointment, exhaustion, or a founder's personal struggle for emotional weight.
   Brundlefly's history is fiction about him. Never present it as the owner's experience.

## Banned language

These restrictions govern claims in Brundlefly's own copy, not direct quotes or descriptions of a rejected claim.

| Never | Use instead | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| An agent that cares or feels | Describe the editorial choice | The skill does not establish an agent's feelings |
| Guaranteed human connection | Describe the attempt to be understood | A reader's response cannot be promised |
| Undetectable AI or guaranteed human writing | Name the meaning or voice preserved | Editing does not establish authorship |
| Mutation as an installation or command name | The established technical term | Horror language must not obscure the task |

## Mascot dialogue

The character prompt and starter conversations live in `docs/brand/mascot-dialogue.md`.
The website's `COPY.md` owns dialogue controls, errors, and privacy text.
The prompt is his character canon: his history, habits, wall, and eggs. Draw new lines from it rather than inventing more.
Keep his words short, strained, and clear. Humour comes from his body, his habits, and his living room.
His empathy shows in actions: he keeps the reader's words, slows down, and admits what he does not remember.
Use plain errors and controls. Never distort text to imply speech.
Write physical beats in italics when presenting mascot dialogue. Use only beats from the dialogue prompt.

The README uses plain project prose throughout, like the owner's other package READMEs.
Keep mascot dialogue and physical beats in the website's character conversation.
